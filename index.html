    How the sim works

    The limits above are certified data. The engine behaviour between them is a teaching model, not a performance model. Assumptions:

    • Shaft power rises with corrected N1 and falls with air density (δ/√θ). It reaches 531 kW at 101.9% N1 at sea level, ISA.
    • TOT follows corrected N1, calibrated so take-off power sits at 845 °C and max continuous near 795 °C.
    • Air mass flow about 2.5 kg/s and pressure ratio about 8 : 1 at take-off. Component efficiencies are 78–85%.
    • Rotor power demand runs from 140 kW (flat pitch) to 600 kW (full collective) at 100% rotor speed.
    • Generic start sequence: ignition at 10% N1, light-off at 12%, starter cut-out at 45%, idle near 68%. Follow the flight manual for real procedures.
    • The governor holds N2 at 100% with a proportional loop plus collective feed-forward. The fuel stop limits N1 to 104.5%.
